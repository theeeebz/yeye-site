Yeye Privacy Policy

Effective 12 November 2026

Yeye is an iPhone app of short guided movement routines, made by Heba Guirguis ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the app keeps, what leaves your phone, and why. In short:

  • Yeye has no accounts. We never ask for your name, email or phone number.
  • What you tell the app, and your session history, stay on your phone.
  • RevenueCat handles the subscription. It receives your purchase history for Yeye and an anonymous ID for your install.
  • Meta hears from Yeye only if you allow tracking. After setup, Yeye asks once, using Apple's tracking prompt. If you allow it, Meta learns whether our ads on Facebook and Instagram led you to install and subscribe. If you don't, nothing about you is sent to Meta.

What stays on your phone

Yeye stores the following on your device only. We never receive any of it:

  • Your answers during setup: your goals, the areas that feel tight (for example neck or lower back), when you like to move, and your reminder time.
  • Your sessions: when you did a routine, how long you moved, and how you said you felt afterwards. This is what builds your streak and your history.
  • Your settings, such as voice cues being on or off.

Daily reminders are scheduled on your phone as local notifications. Voice cues use your phone's built-in speech, which works offline.

Deleting the app deletes all of this data from your phone. If you share your streak, the share sheet sends only what you choose, to the app or person you pick.

Subscriptions (RevenueCat and Apple)

Yeye is sold as a subscription through the App Store, so Apple processes your payment. We never see your card or your Apple Account details.

To check whether your subscription is active, Yeye uses RevenueCat, a subscription service that processes this data on our behalf. RevenueCat receives:

  • an anonymous ID created for your install
  • your App Store purchase history for Yeye
  • basic device information, such as the device model, iOS version, country and IP address
  • whether you allowed tracking

If you allow tracking (see below), RevenueCat also receives your device's advertising identifier, its vendor identifier (an ID Apple gives each app maker) and Meta's anonymous ID for your install, so it can tell Meta about your subscription.

RevenueCat's handling of this data is described in its privacy policy: www.revenuecat.com/privacy.

Ad measurement (Meta), only with your permission

We advertise Yeye on Facebook and Instagram. To learn which ads work, the app includes Meta's software development kit (SDK), and RevenueCat can report subscriptions to Meta. Because Meta can link this to what it already knows about you, Apple calls this tracking, and Yeye asks your permission first with Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt.

If you allow tracking, Meta's SDK sends:

  • when the app is installed and opened
  • an anonymous ID that Meta's SDK creates for your install
  • your device's advertising identifier
  • basic device information, such as the device model, iOS version and IP address

and RevenueCat reports to Meta:

  • that a subscription started or renewed
  • the plan and its price
  • the identifiers above

Meta uses this to tell us which ads led to installs and subscriptions, and to show our ads to people more likely to find Yeye useful. Meta may combine it with other information it holds about you, as described in its own policy.

If you don't allow tracking, Meta's SDK doesn't start and RevenueCat doesn't send anything about you to Meta. Yeye works exactly the same either way.

What Meta never receives from Yeye: your setup answers, the areas you said feel tight, how you felt after a session, your session history, or your name or contact details. We've also switched off the SDK's automatic event logging and its collection of screen details.

You can change your choice at any time in the Settings app, under Privacy & Security, then Tracking. From the next time you open Yeye, it stops sending anything to Meta.

Meta's handling of this data is described in its privacy policy: www.facebook.com/privacy/policy.

Who receives your data

Only RevenueCat and Meta, as described above, and only for those purposes. RevenueCat processes data on our behalf under its data processing terms, and Meta receives it under Meta's Business Tools Terms. We share data only with services that protect it at least as well as this policy does.

  • We don't sell your personal information.
  • We don't send your setup answers or session history to anyone.
  • We don't show ads inside Yeye.
  • We don't use analytics services beyond those named above.

Health information

The areas you say feel tight and how you felt after a session are things you tell the app about your body. They never leave your phone. Yeye offers general movement guidance, not medical advice, and doesn't connect to Apple Health.

Children

Yeye isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect data from them.

How long data is kept

  • On your phone: until you delete the app.
  • At RevenueCat: for as long as needed to provide your subscription and meet legal and accounting requirements.
  • At Meta: under Meta's own retention policy.

Your choices and rights

  • Allow or stop tracking at any time in the Settings app, under Privacy & Security, then Tracking.
  • Delete what's on your phone by deleting the app.
  • Manage or cancel your subscription in Yeye's Settings, or in the Settings app under your name, then Subscriptions.
  • See and clear what Meta received. If you have a Facebook or Instagram account, go to Accounts Center, then Your activity off Meta technologies, to see what apps like Yeye shared, clear it, and disconnect future activity. You can also turn off ad personalization under Ad preferences.
  • Ask about your data or request its deletion. Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or object to the processing of your personal data. Email us at berti.heba@gmail.com. We'll ask RevenueCat to delete its record of your purchases and the identifiers linked to it, and respond within 30 days. Data Meta already holds is cleared through Meta, as described above.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it here and change the date at the top.
